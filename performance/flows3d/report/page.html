3D Torus Flows

Learning action-angle maps for 3D potentials with exactly symplectic shear flows: a toy oscillator torus is mapped onto the true torus, and training asks only that the energy be constant on it.

Where things stand

The test problems

What each stage trains

Every stage learns the same kind of map: (θ, J) → a toy torus with actions J → a 6D symplectic flow → (x, v), trained until each image torus has constant energy in the target potential. The stages differ in the target, the toy, and therefore in what the exact answer is. Units are natural (G = 1); all runs are float64 on the laptop.

Stage 1 · anisotropic oscillator
Target
H = ½|v|² + ½ Σi ωi² xi², with ω = (1, 1.4, 1.9): a different frequency along each axis.
Toy
Cartesian oscillator with the same frequency 1.4 on all three axes: xi = √(2Ji/ω) cos θi, vi = −√(2Jiω) sin θi.
Exact answer
A linear map: stretch each axis by √(1.4/ωi) in x and its inverse in v. The flow's linear layers can represent it exactly.
Ground truth
E(J) = Σ ωi Ji, frequencies ωi.
Training tori
5 per axis, Ji from 10⁻³ to 1 (geometric): 125 tori × 256 angles.
Flow
2 pairs of linear shears + 4 pairs of nonlinear shears of width 16: 684 parameters. Also run with the linear shears alone (36 parameters).
Training
300 Adam steps, then 300 Levenberg–Marquardt steps; 6 minutes.
Stage 2 · separable quartic
Target
H = ½|v|² + Σi (½ ωi² xi² + ¼ λi xi⁴), with ω = (1, 1.4, 1.9) and λ = (1.5, 2.5, 4). Each axis moves independently in its own anharmonic well; at J = 1 the frequencies are 30–57% above ω.
Toy
Cartesian oscillator with frequencies ω = (1, 1.4, 1.9), the target's small-amplitude limit.
Exact answer
Nonlinear, but a product of three independent 1D maps, one per axis. The 6D flow couples all coordinates, so it has to discover that separation.
Ground truth
Each axis's energy Ei(Ji) and frequency by Gauss–Legendre quadrature (round trip good to 4×10⁻¹⁶).
Training tori
Same 125-torus grid × 256 angles. A variant spends the same budget on 1,000 scattered tori × 32 angles.
Flow
2 linear pairs + 8 nonlinear pairs of width 32: 2,612 parameters.
Training
1,000 Adam steps, then 200 LM steps (the variant: 120); 22 minutes.
Stage 3 · isochrone
Target
The isochrone, a spherical potential Φ(r) = −GM / (b + √(b² + r²)), GM = b = 1. A genuinely 3D problem: not separable in Cartesian coordinates, orbits are rosettes in tilted planes. The radial frequency falls 3× across the training range.
Toy
Isotropic oscillator with ω = 0.5 (the isochrone's frequency at its centre), written in spherical actions (Jr, L, Lz): a centred ellipse in a tilted orbital plane. Its tori are labelled the same way as the isochrone's.
Exact answer
A nonlinear map from oscillator tori onto isochrone tori with the same (Jr, L, Lz).
Ground truth
Analytic: H = −(GM)² / (2 [Jr + ½(L + √(L² + 4GMb))]²), with analytic frequencies. On a true torus |x × v| = L everywhere, which gives an independent check.
Training tori
5 per axis in Jr from 10⁻³ to 0.2 and L from 10⁻² to 0.5 (geometric), and cos i from −0.9 to 0.9 (uniform), so Lz = L cos i: 125 tori × 256 angles.
Flow
Same as stage 2: 2,612 parameters.
Training
1,000 Adam steps, then 200 LM steps; 23 minutes.
Common to all stages. Loss: on each torus, the variance of H over its angle samples, divided by the toy energy of that torus, a fixed scale that cannot be gamed. Held-out set: tori halfway (in log J) between the training tori, at 1,024 independent quasi-random angles; never used in training. Reported error: mean of |H − ⟨H⟩| / (⟨H⟩ − Φmin) over the held-out points, so energies are measured from the bottom of the potential. Section 4's experiment starts from the trained stage-3 model and runs 30 more LM steps twice: once with the energy loss alone (the control), once with the energy loss plus the invariance residual.

Held-out energy error during training

Mean relative energy variation on held-out tori (between the training tori, at 1,024 independent angles), against Levenberg–Marquardt step. Each curve starts where its Adam stage ended (300 or 1,000 steps). Errors are relative to the torus energy above the potential minimum.

Which held-out direction fails: new angles or new tori?

Each trained model is scored on its training tori at new angles, and on new tori at its training angles. A large jump on new tori only means the tori are too sparse; a large jump on new angles only would mean too few angles per torus.

4 · First test of a fix

Adding an invariance residual

On a true torus, the system's own motion carries every point along the torus at fixed frequencies: XH(z) = Σi Ωi ∂z/∂θi. The new residual measures how far each point is from that, with Ω fitted per torus by least squares. It is zero for the exact maps above (sanity checks), and on the trained isochrone it is 8× the energy error. Starting from the trained isochrone model, two 30-step Levenberg–Marquardt runs compare the energy loss alone against energy plus invariance (equal weights).

5 · Invariance weight sweep

A small invariance weight helps; a large one hurts

The laptop test used equal weights for 30 steps. This sweep ran on geir, at low priority, from the same trained isochrone model: plain Levenberg–Marquardt with invariance weight β = 0 (the control), 0.01 and 0.1. The invariance residual starts about 8× the energy residual, so β ≈ 1/64 would weigh the two terms equally. The runs were stopped on 19 Oct, once the answer was clear. The weighted runs are much slower per step: they compute the angle tangents and their Jacobian, and geir was heavily loaded. So the curves are compared at equal steps.

Held-out diagnostics against LM step, one curve per weight

6 · Depth against width, finished on an H100

Every run stalls at 1–4×10⁻⁴, and depth doesn’t help

In 1D, depth beat width at equal parameter count: 32 layers reached about 5×10⁻⁷ where 16 stalled near 10⁻⁵. This tests the same question in 3D, on the stage-3 isochrone problem (same toy, 125 training tori × 256 angles, same held-out set), training from scratch rather than fine-tuning. Two flows of about 2,600 parameters each: 16 nonlinear layers 32 wide and 32 layers 16 wide, each trained with the energy loss alone (β = 0) and with the invariance term (β = 0.01), two seeds each. Every run does 1,000 Adam steps, then Levenberg–Marquardt with geodesic acceleration.

All eight ran on geir for 21 h from 2 Nov. By then the β = 0 runs had been flat for over 1,500 steps, and were stopped. The β = 0.01 runs, 5–7× slower per step, moved to one H100 each on 3 Nov. Each continued from its geir checkpoint and stopped on its own once its loss fell by less than 1% over 500 steps.

7 · Width and growing, on H100s

Width is the lever

Section 6 left two explanations for the 1–4×10⁻⁴ stall: too few parameters, or an optimiser that settles early. This separates them. Same isochrone problem, 16 layers, energy loss alone (β = 0), with the layers made 64 and 128 wide (5,172 and 10,292 parameters, against 2,612), two seeds each, all on one H100 per run with the plateau rule.

The runs were started two ways. From scratch, as in section 6. And grown: each trained 32-wide flow is widened, with the new units’ output weights set to zero, so the grown flow starts as exactly the same map as its source (checked: the two agree to the last digit, and the held-out error at step 0 is the source’s). Any improvement after that comes from the added units.

8 · Next benchmark: realistic halos

From an isochrone to an NFW halo and to MWPotential2014

The isochrone target has a closed-form answer, which made it a good first 3D problem and a poor stand-in for a galaxy. This benchmark keeps everything about the method and changes the two ends of the map. The toy is now the isochrone itself, through its exact action-angle map, so the flow starts from orbits that already look like rosettes. The targets are an NFW halo, which is still spherical and so still has an exact answer to score against, and MWPotential2014, which is flattened by its disc and has none.

NFW halo
Target
Φ(r) = −A ln(1 + r/a) / r, with a = 16 kpc and A set so that vc(8 kpc) = 220 km/s. Its density rises as 1/r towards the centre, where the isochrone has a flat core.
Exact answer
A map onto NFW tori with the same (Jr, L, Lz). Not known in closed form.
Ground truth
H(Jr, L) and both frequencies by quadrature of the radial motion, good to about 10⁻¹⁰ (checks below). On a true torus |x × v| = L.
Toy
MWPotential2014
Target
Bovy (2015), as in galpy: a power-law bulge with a cut-off, a Miyamoto–Nagai disc (a = 3 kpc, b = 0.28 kpc) and an NFW halo (a = 16 kpc), carrying 5%, 60% and 35% of vc² at 8 kpc. Axisymmetric, not spherical.
Exact answer
Tori with actions (JR, Lz, Jz). A toy torus (Jr, L, Lz) lands on the one with JR = Jr and Jz = L − |Lz|. There may be resonant regions where no smooth map exists.
Ground truth
No exact H(J). What can be measured without one: held-out energy variance, the invariance residual, and Lz, which is constant on every true torus (|L| is not).
Toy
Common to both. Flow and training: 16 layers 32 wide from scratch (1,000 Adam steps, then LM with the plateau rule), then grown to 128 wide, the recipe from section 7. Energy loss alone, two seeds. Units are galpy’s natural ones: lengths in units of 8 kpc, velocities in units of 220 km/s, so an action of 1 is 1,760 kpc km/s.
