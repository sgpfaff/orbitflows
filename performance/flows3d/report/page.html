3D Torus Flows

Learning action-angle maps for 3D potentials with exactly symplectic shear flows: a toy oscillator torus is mapped onto the true torus, and training asks only that the energy be constant on it.

The test problems

What each stage trains

Every stage learns the same kind of map: (θ, J) → a toy torus with actions J → a 6D symplectic flow → (x, v), trained until each image torus has constant energy in the target potential. The stages differ in the target, the toy, and therefore in what the exact answer is. Units are natural (G = 1); all runs are float64 on the laptop.

Stage 1 · anisotropic oscillator
Target
H = ½|v|² + ½ Σi ωi² xi², with ω = (1, 1.4, 1.9): a different frequency along each axis.
Toy
Cartesian oscillator with the same frequency 1.4 on all three axes: xi = √(2Ji/ω) cos θi, vi = −√(2Jiω) sin θi.
Exact answer
A linear map: stretch each axis by √(1.4/ωi) in x and its inverse in v. The flow's linear layers can represent it exactly.
Ground truth
E(J) = Σ ωi Ji, frequencies ωi.
Training tori
5 per axis, Ji from 10⁻³ to 1 (geometric): 125 tori × 256 angles.
Flow
2 pairs of linear shears + 4 pairs of nonlinear shears of width 16: 684 parameters. Also run with the linear shears alone (36 parameters).
Training
300 Adam steps, then 300 Levenberg–Marquardt steps; 6 minutes.
Stage 2 · separable quartic
Target
H = ½|v|² + Σi (½ ωi² xi² + ¼ λi xi⁴), with ω = (1, 1.4, 1.9) and λ = (1.5, 2.5, 4). Each axis moves independently in its own anharmonic well; at J = 1 the frequencies are 30–57% above ω.
Toy
Cartesian oscillator with frequencies ω = (1, 1.4, 1.9), the target's small-amplitude limit.
Exact answer
Nonlinear, but a product of three independent 1D maps, one per axis. The 6D flow couples all coordinates, so it has to discover that separation.
Ground truth
Each axis's energy Ei(Ji) and frequency by Gauss–Legendre quadrature (round trip good to 4×10⁻¹⁶).
Training tori
Same 125-torus grid × 256 angles. A variant spends the same budget on 1,000 scattered tori × 32 angles.
Flow
2 linear pairs + 8 nonlinear pairs of width 32: 2,612 parameters.
Training
1,000 Adam steps, then 200 LM steps (the variant: 120); 22 minutes.
Stage 3 · isochrone
Target
The isochrone, a spherical potential Φ(r) = −GM / (b + √(b² + r²)), GM = b = 1. A genuinely 3D problem: not separable in Cartesian coordinates, orbits are rosettes in tilted planes. The radial frequency falls 3× across the training range.
Toy
Isotropic oscillator with ω = 0.5 (the isochrone's frequency at its centre), written in spherical actions (Jr, L, Lz): a centred ellipse in a tilted orbital plane. Its tori are labelled the same way as the isochrone's.
Exact answer
A nonlinear map from oscillator tori onto isochrone tori with the same (Jr, L, Lz).
Ground truth
Analytic: H = −(GM)² / (2 [Jr + ½(L + √(L² + 4GMb))]²), with analytic frequencies. On a true torus |x × v| = L everywhere, which gives an independent check.
Training tori
5 per axis in Jr from 10⁻³ to 0.2 and L from 10⁻² to 0.5 (geometric), and cos i from −0.9 to 0.9 (uniform), so Lz = L cos i: 125 tori × 256 angles.
Flow
Same as stage 2: 2,612 parameters.
Training
1,000 Adam steps, then 200 LM steps; 23 minutes.
Common to all stages. Loss: on each torus, the variance of H over its angle samples, divided by the toy energy of that torus, a fixed scale that cannot be gamed. Held-out set: tori halfway (in log J) between the training tori, at 1,024 independent quasi-random angles; never used in training. Reported error: mean of |H − ⟨H⟩| / (⟨H⟩ − Φmin) over the held-out points, so energies are measured from the bottom of the potential. Section 4's experiment starts from the trained stage-3 model and runs 30 more LM steps twice: once with the energy loss alone (the control), once with the energy loss plus the invariance residual.

2 · Validation

Held-out error, and where it comes from

Held-out energy error during training

Mean relative energy variation on held-out tori (between the training tori, at 1,024 independent angles), against Levenberg–Marquardt step. Each curve starts where its Adam stage ended (300 or 1,000 steps). Errors are relative to the torus energy above the potential minimum.

Which held-out direction fails: new angles or new tori?

Each trained model is scored on its training tori at new angles, and on new tori at its training angles. A large jump on new tori only means the tori are too sparse; a large jump on new angles only would mean too few angles per torus.

4 · First test of a fix

Adding an invariance residual

On a true torus, the system's own motion carries every point along the torus at fixed frequencies: XH(z) = Σi Ωi ∂z/∂θi. The new residual measures how far each point is from that, with Ω fitted per torus by least squares. It is zero for the exact maps above (sanity checks), and on the trained isochrone it is 8× the energy error. Starting from the trained isochrone model, two 30-step Levenberg–Marquardt runs compare the energy loss alone against energy plus invariance (equal weights).
